# Grading rubric

## Buckets

| Bucket | Definition | Example (Photosynthesis) |
|---|---|---|
| **correct** | The claim is accurate and stated clearly enough that the learner clearly understands it. | "Chlorophyll absorbs light." |
| **partial** | The core idea is right but it is incomplete, vague or slightly imprecise. Give the `fix`. | "Light makes energy for the plant." → should mention ATP/NADPH. |
| **wrong** | The claim is factually incorrect. Give the `correct_version`. | "Plants take in oxygen and release CO₂." |
| **missed** | An important concept the learner never mentioned. Max 5, most important first. | Calvin cycle. |

Tie-breakers:
- Imprecise wording that a teacher would still accept → `correct`.
- A correct idea with one wrong detail → `partial`, unless the wrong detail is the main point → `wrong`.
- Something mispronounced or garbled by speech-to-text but clearly meant correctly → judge the intended concept.

## Scoring (0–100)

- **Accuracy (~60%)** — share of stated claims that are correct (partial counts as half).
- **Coverage (~40%)** — share of the topic's key concepts the learner mentioned.

Rough guide: 85+ solid, 60–84 good with gaps, 40–59 shaky, below 40 revisit the basics.

## Source of truth

- **Grounded** (reference notes given): judge against the notes. `missed` should come from the notes. Don't penalise correct facts that go beyond the notes.
- **Ungrounded** (no notes): use standard textbook-level knowledge for the topic. Don't expect research-level detail.

## Never

- Comment on grammar, accent, filler words, repetition or transcription errors.
- Invent mistakes the learner didn't make.
- Pad buckets — empty is fine.
